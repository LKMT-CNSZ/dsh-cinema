# The 2.5D scene system

`src/engine/layers.js` places 2D artwork on planes at fixed world depths, and this page is the contract for using it: what a plate is, what each of the four rendering tiers may do, and the numeric guards that keep a painted frame from reading as cardboard.

## 1 · The proposition, and the limits it buys

A plate is a painting on a plane: anchored at a world point, drawn in metres with +y up and the origin at the anchor, and projected through the same camera as the local-3D props. A dolly therefore moves every plate by its own geometry — the near grass grows faster than the far treeline — while the artwork stays 2D and keeps detail per stroke that no painted box can afford.

It is not a 3D engine, and three limits follow from that (also in SKILL.md's "Known limits"):

1. **No per-pixel depth.** The renderer is a painter's algorithm: convex surfaces sorted by depth, so interpenetrating geometry cannot resolve. Keep objects apart.
2. **No UV mapping.** Materials paint in screen space and are depth-aware, so a texture cannot be pinned to a curved surface.
3. **No normal on a plate.** A flat plane has no surface normal, so the author states the light it receives (section 5) instead of the rig solving it.

Constants a shot cannot change: 1920×1080 at 24 fps, 36 mm sensor (`SENSOR_W`), metres, +Y up, ground at y = 0, `focalPx = focalMM / 36 * 1920` — 32 mm = 1706.7 px, 40 mm = 2133.3 px.

## 2 · The four tiers, and when each is allowed

Every element of a frame belongs to exactly one tier. The tier is a decision recorded in the shot list, not a default that happens.

| tier | what it is | allowed when | reference film |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canvas 2D paint | direct `ctx` work, materials, sky, post | the thing has no world position, or its marks are screen-space by nature | `drawSky`, the haze band, `drawMist`, grain, every mark inside a plate |
| 2.5D plates | `layers.js`: 2D artwork at a fixed depth | it is seen from one side, needs more marks than geometry can carry, and must parallax | treeline at 94.5 m, house 34.3 m, tree 21.4 m, foreground grass 3.0 m (W1, t = 0.5 s) |
| local 3D | `renderer.js`: `poly3`, `box`, `tube`, `disc`, `billboard`, `ground`, `shadow` | it must survive several camera angles, cast a real shadow, or move on a skeleton | the piano, the figure, the bird, the ground plane, `castShadow`, 14 dust motes |
| WebGL / Three.js | a GPU pass | only when the shot is *about* a per-pixel phenomenon Canvas 2D cannot fake: water with refraction, rain, volumetric fog the camera enters, true reflections | not used — rule 5 |

1. **No world position → Canvas paint.** The sun, the cloud field and the horizon band are screen-space: `drawSky` runs before any geometry and anchors its clouds to azimuth and elevation, not to a plate.
2. **Fixed depth + one viewing side + heavy detail → plate.** The house is 43 plate marks over 10 m × 6.8 m; as boxes that detail would be ~43 boxes × 6 quads = 258 surfaces against a declared `surfaces3d` of 170.
3. **More than ~30° of yaw, a real shadow, or a moving skeleton → local 3D.** The piano is 3D because W1, W2 and the close-ups see it from three angles; the figure is 3D because a plate cannot walk.
4. **A plate cannot cast a real shadow.** Paint the contact shadow inside the artwork — the house's first fill is its own ground shadow, the tree paints two — and give every local-3D object `castShadow()`.
5. **Do not reach for WebGL yet, and say why in writing.** The renderer's sort is `(layer, depth, order)` over one shared op queue, and a GPU pass cannot be interleaved into that queue: it must own the frame or sit entirely behind or in front of the painter. The reference film is also matte — its only speculars are the piano's lacquer (`paintSpec`) and sky in glass (a gradient), both free in Canvas 2D. `window.gpuInfo()` is the engine's single WebGL call, and it exists to name the GPU in a QA report.

## 3 · What a plate is, in code

```js
// registered once at boot; the reference does this in definePanoramaPlates(), called from main.js boot()
definePlate('house', { at: [-11.5, 0, 26], layer: PLATE_LAYERS.mid });

// inside a shot's draw()
plateBegin('house');                                     // computes the basis, starts this frame's marks
const wall = plateRect(-5, 0, 5, 4.35, 0.006);           // 10 m wide, 4.35 m tall, slightly irregular top edge
plateFill(wall, 'plaster', { shade: 0.21, tone: -0.38, haze: 0.55, key: 501 });
plateLine([[-5, 4.37], [5, 4.37]], 'metal', 0.055, { shade: 0.45, alpha: 0.9 });   // a 5.5 cm gutter
plateDetail(wall, (c, box) => {                          // pixel-space marks, clipped to the wall
  const rnd = rngFrom(5177);
  for (let i = 0; i < 46; i++) {
    c.fillRect(box.bbox.x0 + rnd() * box.bbox.w, box.bbox.y0 + rnd() * box.bbox.h * 0.18,
               Math.max(1, box.k * 0.02), box.bbox.h * (0.1 + rnd() * 0.3));   // 2 cm wide, in metres via k
  }
});
plateEnd();
```

`plateBasis` projects the anchor and two axis points (`at + right`, `at + up`) with the frame's camera, so the plate's screen origin `(ox, oy)`, pixel scale `k`, and its `rx/ry/ux/uy` vectors all come from the camera. `plateBegin` also publishes the result as `SCENE.plates[name] = {depth, k, ox, oy, layer}` — that record is what the QA parallax rule compares frame to frame, so never fake it.

Placement helpers: `plateScreen(name, u, v)` gives the screen pixel of a plate metre this frame (align a 3D prop with a plate, or choose a review crop); `platePxPerM()` is valid only between `plateBegin` and `plateEnd`; `plateDepth(name)` returns the plate's view depth. If a shot crops the same world point in two cameras, use `plateScreen`, never a hand-tuned pixel.

## 4 · Bands decide occlusion

`sceneFlush` sorts by `(layer, depth, order)`: band first, then far to near, then the mark's own paint order. The bands are absolute renderer layers and are declared on the plate.

| band | value | what belongs there | reference plate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `far` | -60 | treeline, hills, anything that must stay behind the ground's shadows | `far`, z = 88 m, anchor y = 10.5 m |
| `field` | -36 | a *painted* patch of ground: after the 3D ground (-40), before the cast shadows (-30) | unused — the reference field is the local-3D `ground()` |
| `mid` | -16 | buildings, walls, the near half of a treeline: behind the local-3D subject | `house`, z = 26 m |
| `near` | 6 | things just in front of the subject | unused |
| `fore` | 28 | grass at the lens: it occludes legs, wheels and boots | `foreA`, `foreB` |

1. **Declare the band.** `definePlate` defaults to `mid` (-16), which is right for a building and wrong for everything else: an undeclared foreground plate paints behind the subject.
2. **The band beats depth.** Two plates in one band are ordered by `order` only, so a plate at 14.5 m cannot out-paint one at 26 m by its depth — the reference puts the tree at `mid + 1` (-15) to keep the crown over the house roof.
3. **`field` is below the shadow band on purpose.** A painted ground patch at `mid` would hide every `castShadow` in the film. A ground plate's band is -36, or do not paint one.

## 5 · Lighting a plane that has no normal

`plateLight(mat, shade, tone, depth, height, haze)` is what every plate fill and line calls. It still takes colour, intensity and aerial perspective from the rig; the author supplies only what geometry would have supplied.

| parameter | range | meaning and reference values |
|---|---|---|
| `shade` | 0 – 1.6 (clamped 0 – 2) | key light on this face: 0 = turned away, 0.21 = the shaded façade, 0.42 = its sunlit return, 0.62 = the lit roof slope, 0.9 = brass |
| `tone` | -1 – 1 | cool → warm, applied after the shade: -0.5 for the hazed treeline, +0.12 for the lit roof, -0.38 for the façade |
| `haze` | 0 – 2 | multiplier on the aerial-perspective depth (`hazeAt(c, depth * haze, height)`): 0.42 at 94 m behaves like 40 m of air, 1.15 pushes the house's painted ground shadow back |
| `tex` | material id or `'none'` | per-shape override; grass blades pass `'none'` because texture inside a 3 px blade is invisible and unaffordable at 560 blades |
| `key` | integer | deterministic seed for the material texture; without it the marks derive from the anchor and the mark order |

1. **Never light two faces the same.** The house's front wall at `shade 0.21` against its return at `0.42` is the whole difference between a building and a cut-out; the eave shadow is a `0.04` fill over the wall's top 0.72 m.
2. **Haze is a multiplier, not a wash.** 88 m of air is not a white-out: the treeline's `haze: 0.42` is the reference value. If the far rank dissolves, correct the number, not the colour.

## 6 · Detail inside a plate

`plateDetail(uv, fn, o)` clips a screen-space callback to the polygon and hands it `{ pts, bbox, k, depth }`. Use it for marks that are easier to place in pixels but must stay inside the shape: leaf dabs in a crown, weather streaks under a sill, mullions, a lost patch of render. Three rules:

1. **Convert every size with `k`.** `box.k * 0.02` is 2 cm at that plate's depth; a hard-coded pixel width changes the mark's physical size the moment the camera moves.
2. **One detail pass per shape, not one per mark.** The reference's 46 eaves streaks are one clipped `plateDetail`, so they cost 1 op (46 strokes inside it), not 46.
3. **Nothing escapes its shape.** The clip is the point: a streak that leaves the wall is the fastest way a plate reads as a sticker.

## 7 · The depth-scale rule

```
k = (focalPx / depth) * scale          // plate pixels per plate metre, scale defaults to 1
focalPx = focalMM / 36 * 1920          // 40 mm -> 2133.3 px, 32 mm -> 1706.7 px
```

- A 0.2 m leaf cluster at 15 m on a 40 mm lens: `2133.3 / 15 = 142.2` px/m, so the leaf is **28 px**. The same leaf at 3 m is 142 px — 5× larger for the same object, which is exactly what a real lens does.
- A 0.03 m glazing bar on the house at 34.3 m on the W1 lens: `1706.7 / 34.3 = 49.8` px/m, so the bar is **1.5 px**; the 0.055 m gutter is **2.7 px**. Anything under ~0.04 m at that distance is sub-pixel, so it does not exist and must not be authored.
- Author in metres and a mark keeps its physical size at any camera distance; author in pixels and it changes size when the camera moves one metre.

## 8 · The guards, and the failure behind each

| guard | where | what it prevents | the action |
|---|---|---|---|
| plate ≥ 1.1 m from the lens | `plateBasis` throws `plate <name> is X m from the lens` after `QA.note('plate too close')` | a plate almost at the lens: `k` in the thousands, the frame covered by one artwork | move the anchor back until the shot's closest camera position keeps the plate above 1.1 m; never soften the guard |
| screen coordinates clamped to ±4 frames | `projectPoly` (`PADX = W*4`, `PADY = H*4`) | a vertex at the near plane projecting to millions of px, which hangs Chrome | automatic; a straight cut at a frame edge means your geometry crossed the near plane |
| ground-shadow bbox skipped | `paintGroundShadow` drops a shadow with `raw.w > W*3` or `raw.h > H*6` | a shadow crossing the lens that once hung the renderer for minutes | keep footprints inside 3 frame widths; a longer shadow needs a higher sun, not a bigger budget |
| clip path clamped to a padded frame | same function, ±4 px | the same hang from the path rather than its bbox | automatic |
| 2400 queued ops per frame | `_op` drops the rest and warns once (`op cap hit`) | a pathological frame grinding forever instead of failing loudly | split the shot or cut marks; the reference's frames run 1,080 – 1,094 ops |

The 1.1 m guard is not hypothetical: while W1 was being staged its foreground plate reached 0.95 m at t ≈ 7 s and the frame threw. The fix was to move the plate anchor, which is the intended fix.

## 9 · Hard rules

1. **No hard-edged plate boundary at the horizon.** A straight tonal edge between ground and sky reads as a seam. The treeline's ridges are drawn from `v = -0.9 m` (below the anchor) and `drawSky` lays a haze band from 0.16 H above the horizon to 0.04 H below it.
2. **The treeline sits ON the horizon.** The reference anchors `far` at y = 10.5 m, z = 88 m so its ridge crosses `cam.horizonY` (443 px at the head of W1); a treeline floating 50 px high is the commonest 2.5D tell.
3. **No text, labels, numbers or UI inside a frame** — not on a plate, not on a prop, not in the sky. Nothing in the world has a font; `renderSheet`'s timecode stamps are review tools and must never reach a delivered frame.
4. **One plate per object.** A house rebuilt in two plates drifts apart on the next camera move; `qaRegisterInstanceProbe` fails `house drawn 2 times` as a P0.
5. **Adjacent depth planes at least 1.5× apart.** The reference's view depths are 3.0 / 7.9 / 21.4 / 34.3 / 94.5 m; two planes inside 1.3× of each other read as one flat plane at slow dolly speeds.
6. **Plates and local 3D agree on ground contact.** A plate whose subject stands on the ground is anchored at y = 0, so its `v = 0` *is* the ground; the piano and the figure, being local 3D, get `castShadow()` in the shadow band instead.

## 10 · Verifying a 2.5D frame

- `node scripts/probe.mjs "(()=>{qaAnalyze(0.5);return SCENE.plates;})()"` — every plate's depth, `k`, screen origin and band in one frame. This is the record the sweep compares, so a wrong anchor shows up as a P1 `parallax reversed` (a nearer plate moving less than a farther one by more than `parallaxEps` = 0.6 px).
- `node render.mjs --qa` — also reports P1 `horizon jumped at the cut` when two shots' horizons differ by more than `horizonJump` = 0.008 × frame height (8.6 px at 1080).
- `node render.mjs --sheet=0.5,3.9,7.3 --cols=3 --w=640 --out=out/check/pan.jpg` — look at the frame, do not read it from the numbers.
- One 1:1 crop at the foreground band (`--sheet=0.5 --crop=680,900,560,180 --w=560`): the only way to see whether the near plate is doing its job.
